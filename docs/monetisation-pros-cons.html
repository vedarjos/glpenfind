GLPenFind — thirteen ways this could earn, and what each one costs you

Net of 20% VAT and Apple’s 15% Small Business rate where a store sale is involved. Customer counts are the thresholds at which each idea pays, not forecasts. Green border means worth doing; dashed red means don’t.

One pageNovember 2026
01

Quarterly report sold to the industry

Price by dose, spread, movers, ordering-friction trend — sold to clinics, wholesalers, researchers.

Pros

  • Best value per hour — no new code
  • Ten buyers is a viable business
  • Journalists cite you free

Cons

  • You must invoice and support
  • Stale data kills it fast
  • Maybe 30 UK buyers, total
£450 a copy · 10 = £4,500/yrDo this
02

Licence the live data as an API

£150/month per key for current prices and Ease scores, for clinics and comparison sites.

Pros

  • Two clients = £3,600/yr
  • Recurring, runs itself
  • No extra crawling

Cons

  • Keys, limits, docs, uptime
  • Breaks when a site changes
  • You become a support desk
£150/mo · 5 = £9,000/yrDo this
03

Pharmacies claim their profile

£25/month for logo, hours, delivery terms and a correction route — cosmetics only.

Pros

  • 10 of 68 = £3,000/yr
  • They fix our errors
  • Direct supplier contact

Cons

  • Looks like paid placement
  • One angry pharmacy is a story
  • Invoicing for small sums
£25/mo · 10 = £3,000/yrFirewall first
04

Free app, £2.99 Pro unlock

Alerts, widget, reorder reminders, price history — things a web page cannot do.

Pros

  • £2.12 net, no renewals
  • Free download grows ranking
  • Native work answers rule 4.2

Cons

  • Native features must exist first
  • Few pay for a price list
  • Apple takes 15%
£2.99 · 1,400 = £2,968Best app model
05

Web membership, £3/month

Alerts, history, watchlist, CSV export — billed by Stripe, no Apple cut.

Pros

  • 100 members = £3,420/yr
  • No 15% to Apple
  • Recurring if it sticks

Cons

  • Accounts, billing, support
  • 300 members is a lot alone
  • Must not unlock the app
£3/mo · 300 = £10,260/yrLater
06

£0.99 paid app, up front

Pay before download, everything included.

Pros

  • No billing code needed
  • Filters tyre-kickers
  • One decision, done

Cons

  • £0.70 net — needs volume
  • Most won’t pay blind
  • Fewer users, worse ranking
  • Paid wrapper risks 4.2
5,000 = £3,506 · break-even 113Weakest
07

£1.99/year subscription

Annual fee for the app and its alerts.

Pros

  • £1.41/yr each, repeating
  • Reframes it as a service

Cons

  • Churn on a £2 product is brutal
  • “Why pay for public prices?”
  • Contradicts anti-hassle pitch
56 subs covers Apple’s feeMeh
08

Reader donations

“This saved me £40 a month” plus a tip jar.

Pros

  • Ten minutes to set up
  • Signals independence
  • 80 = £3,840/yr

Cons

  • Unpredictable
  • Needs an audience first
  • Feels like asking
£4/mo · 80 = £3,840/yrEasy, low risk
09

Licence an embedded price box

“Cheapest 15mg today — £220” for forums and clinic sites.

Pros

  • £20–50/mo a site, pure margin
  • Every embed advertises you
  • Reaches people off-site

Cons

  • Needs an endpoint and cache
  • Easy to block or ignore
  • Few willing hosts
£20–50/mo per siteLater
10

Display advertising

Banner networks on the price list.

Pros

  • Trivial to add
  • Pays without selling

Cons

  • ~£50/mo at this traffic
  • Undoes the clean look
  • Health ad networks = privacy risk
Pennies per thousand viewsAvoid
11

Paid placement or a “featured” slot

A pharmacy buys the top of the list.

Pros

  • Would earn the most, fastest

Cons

  • Ends the whole premise
  • Ease score becomes meaningless
  • MHRA risk over POM inducements
  • Unrecoverable
Never, at any priceNever
12

Sell access to the list

Put the index behind the app or a login.

Pros

  • Forces a purchase from visitors

Cons

  • Kills the search traffic
  • Charges for what Monj gives free
  • The advert loses its audience
Trades the asset for a receiptNo
13

Affiliate links to pharmacies

Commission per referral — already excluded by the project’s rules.

Pros

  • Standard for comparison sites
  • Costs the reader nothing

Cons

  • Incentive to rank payers higher
  • Readers cannot audit it
  • Contradicts stated independence
Excluded by designNo

If any pharmacy ever pays, these four rules go public first

  1. Ranking is a pure function — scraped price and counted steps. No manual overrides, ever.
  2. A public “who pays us” page, updated the day an invoice is raised.
  3. Payment buys cosmetics only: logo, hours, a link — never position, price or Ease.
  4. Nothing paid appears in the app’s ranking either. One rule, both surfaces.
