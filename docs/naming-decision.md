# Name decision — 31 Oct 2026

**Chosen: GLPenFind** (typeset exactly so: `GLP` caps, `en` lowercase, `Find` capital F).

## Why it won

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary | `glp` 9–11 per 10k words in UK subs (higher than the global sub); `pen` 29–42 per 10k — the top object word |
| Collision | No exact use anywhere on the web or in the GB App Store; the only near-neighbours are GLP-1 trackers (*PenShot*, *Penwise*, *GLPer*) |
| Compliance | Names a drug **class**, not a brand — no MHRA objection to a URL, no trademark confusion with a POM |
| Sayable | Weakest area: the `P` belongs to both `GLP` and `pen` |
| Domains | Every TLD free (verified by RDAP, with a control group confirming the checker) |

## Rejected

- **PenPriceUK / PenFind** — "pen price" and "pen find" both read as stationery in the App Store and on Google; `pen price uk` still returned handwriting apps.
- **MnJroFind** — vowel-dropped encoding of the brand: MHRA URL exposure + unsayable.
- **DoseCompare** — `compare` appears 3 per 10k words; it is marketer language, not patient language.
- **GLP-Find** — hyphen is a typing tax and `find` carries no promise on its own.

## Register

`glpenfind.com` · `glpenfind.co.uk` · `glpenfind.uk` · `glpenfind.app`, plus `glp-penfind.com` for people who hyphenate what they hear.

## Mitigations for the spelling trap

1. Always typeset `GLPenFind` — the caps are the only cue to where GLP ends.
2. Own `glp-penfind.com` as a redirect.
3. Let the subtitle carry the category: `UK weight-loss pen prices`.
4. Keyword field still claims the drug term invisibly: `mounjaro,tirzepatide,glp1,...`
